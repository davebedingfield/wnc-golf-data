One of WNC’s most storied resort courses, where golf was first played in 1899. The course was redesigned by Donald Ross in 1926. Bobby Jones, Snead, Hogan, Nicklaus, and multiple U.S. Presidents have all played here, making it the most historically significant resort course in the region. No driving range on property, so use the resort’s outdoor hitting bays and practice putting green to dial in before your round.Don’t let the scorecard fool you on the short par-4 6th hole; members consistently rank it among the hardest on the course. On the greens, read almost everything as breaking away from the hotel. Holes 1 and 18 are the exceptions.

The original Karl Litten layout was completely rebuilt following catastrophic Hurricane Helene flooding in 2024, and several holes were rerouted. The fairways track alongside natural creeks and ponds, framed by the Blue Ridge scenery. The renovated routing features wider landing corridors and strategic bunkering around the new bentgrass green complexes.The new routing makes old yardage books completely obsolete. The updated practice facility is a major upgrade — a massive modern hitting structure anchors a fully expanded short game area. Get there early and use it; the bentgrass greens reward players who dial in their distances before the round.

Bob Cupp’s 1986 design, built with bentgrass tee-to-green at 4,000 feet, is widely considered one of the most challenging mountain courses in WNC — a distinction backed by the Asheville Citizen-Times. While formerly entirely private, the club now welcomes limited public tee times — book well in advance.Afternoon slots are the sweet spot for value. The 12th is a jaw-dropping par 4: A massive vertical drop from the elevated tee box down to the fairway, 416 yards from the tips. Precise iron placements are how locals navigate the blind shots and score well.

Robert Trent Jones II’s 2009 design on Eastern Band Cherokee land is the most awarded public-access mountain course in WNC. It appears on Golfweek’s top casino courses list, and tee shots drop hundreds of feet throughout the layout — a thrilling roller coaster ride from the first tee to the 18th green. Smoky Mountain National Park provides the backdrop on every hole.Pair the round with a stay at Harrah’s Cherokee Casino Resort, just 10 minutes away, for the ultimate WNC golf-and-gaming getaway.
